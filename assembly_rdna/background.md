# Background: 45S rDNA in long-read human genome assemblies

Current HiFi plus ultra-long nanopore assemblies of human genomes contain rDNA sequence but almost never the rDNA arrays. No automated assembler has produced a complete, correctly sized 45S array except where the array is only a few units long. Every complete human reference has instead used model sequence (T2T-CHM13), sized N-gaps (T2T-HG002) or targeted reconstruction of single arrays. For HPRC release 2 (r2), whose haplotypes are almost all hifiasm assemblies, rDNA should therefore appear mainly as fragments, collapsed units and unplaced contigs, and assembled unit counts should not be expected to track true copy number. The sections below give the biology, technology and precedents behind this expectation, then list features that separate a faithful array from a collapsed or templated one.

## rDNA biology relevant to assembly

### Unit structure and array location

Each human 45S rDNA unit is roughly 43–45 kb long. The ~13 kb transcribed region yields the 18S, 5.8S and 28S rRNAs, and the remainder is an intergenic spacer (IGS) of about 32 kb ([Lin et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2026.05.035); [Stults et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1101/gr.6858507)). The current reference unit, GenBank KY962518, was derived from TAR-cloned chromosome 21 rDNA sequenced with long reads. It is 44,838 bp long and carries an IGS segment of about 2 kb that is missing from the older U13369.1 reference ([Kim et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gky442)). Units are arranged head-to-tail in arrays at 13p12, 14p12, 15p12, 21p12 and 22p12 ([Stults et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1101/gr.6858507)), giving ten arrays in a diploid genome. In T2T-CHM13 every short arm has the same layout: an rDNA array embedded between distal and proximal repeat-rich sequence. The distal junction (DJ) next to the array contains centromere-like repeats and a conserved, transcribed palindrome of about 200 kb ([Nurk et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.abj6987)). An end-to-end TAR clone of one chromosome 22 nucleolar organiser contained only two rDNA units, together with the proximal (PJ) and distal junctions ([Kim et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-021-82565-x)). Arrays at the small end of the size range therefore do exist. The 5S rRNA genes lie in a separate array of 2.2 kb units at 1q42; Stults et al. measured an average of 98 repeats ([Stults et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1101/gr.6858507)). The 5S array is not considered further here.

### Copy number and array size

Total 45S copy number is in the hundreds, and it differs between individuals and between homologous chromosomes. The T2T consortium cited an average of 315 copies per diploid genome, with a standard deviation of 104 copies ([Nurk et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.abj6987), citing [Parks et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aao0665)). Another survey reports that the five arrays together typically encode 200–600 units ([Lin et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2026.05.035)). Antonarakis summarised short-read estimates as roughly 30 units per acrocentric chromosome on average ([Antonarakis 2022](https://doi.org/10.1101/gr.275350.121)). CHM13 has about 400 copies by Illumina depth and 409 ± 9 by ddPCR ([Nurk et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.abj6987)). Ten HPRC lymphoblastoid lines ranged from over 400 to over 600 copies in total ([Potapova et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xgen.2025.101031)), and a three-generation pedigree carried 554–625 units ([Cechova et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.64898/2025.12.14.693655)).

Individual arrays vary far more than the totals. Pulsed-field cluster lengths range from 50 kb to more than 6 Mb, with almost every individual heterozygous for cluster length ([Stults et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1101/gr.6858507)). By FISH, 100 arrays from 10 HPRC individuals ranged from more than 150 units to 1–2 units or none, and four arrays carried 0–2 copies; some whole arrays were silenced by methylation, heritably ([Potapova et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xgen.2025.101031)). CHM13 arrays span 0.7 Mb (chr14) to 3.6 Mb (chr13), and the chr15 array carries a heterozygous deletion of about 1 Mb ([Nurk et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.abj6987)).

### Instability and inter-chromosomal exchange

rDNA arrays are recombinationally unstable. Stults et al. followed cluster lengths through multigenerational families and estimated meiotic rearrangement at more than 10% per cluster per meiosis ([Stults et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1101/gr.6858507)). In somatic tissue, over half of the adult solid tumours they later examined showed rDNA rearrangements not present in matched normal tissue ([Stults et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1158/0008-5472.can-09-2680)). Recent family studies qualify this: FISH-based array sizes passed through at least one generation without large changes ([Potapova et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xgen.2025.101031)), and per-array estimates in a T2T pedigree correlated at r = 0.78 across generations, with most changes in the largest, least precisely measured arrays ([Cechova et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.64898/2025.12.14.693655)). Whether the two views reconcile through small changes below imaging resolution is unresolved. Either way, array sizes in a cell-line assembly need not match the donor.

Sequence is also exchanged between the short arms of different acrocentric chromosomes. In an all-to-all comparison of HPRC release 1 contigs, the short arms clustered together as a single community. Guarracino et al. defined pseudo-homologous regions (PHRs) totalling 18.3 Mb in which contigs look like recombinants of heterologous chromosomes, with the highest signal right next to the rDNA arrays ([Guarracino et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-05976-y)). Within the rDNA itself, CHM13 units differ more between chromosomes than within an array. This points to intra-chromosomal homogenisation as the dominant process, and it produces chromosome-specific variants that can be used to assign units ([Nurk et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.abj6987)). Allelic recombination on the short arms, by contrast, is depleted: one pedigree study of 107 transmissions found only a single ectopic chr13–chr21 exchange there ([Lin et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2026.05.035)).

## Sequencing and assembly technology

### Read types and phasing data

PacBio HiFi reads reached 99.8% accuracy at an average length of 13.5 kb when first described ([Wenger et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41587-019-0217-9)), so a HiFi read is shorter than one rDNA unit. Oxford Nanopore ultra-long protocols give read N50 above 100 kb, with individual reads up to 882 kb ([Jain et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/nbt.4060)). That is enough to span a few units, but the arrays are megabases long and exceed ultra-long read lengths ([Antipov et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1101/gr.280383.124)). HPRC r2 samples have about 60× HiFi and 30× nanopore coverage in reads over 100 kb, plus Hi-C and Illumina data ([IGSR HPRC r2 data page](https://www.internationalgenome.org/data-portal/data-collections/hprc2.html)). Phasing comes from one of two external sources: parental short-read k-mers (trio mode) or Hi-C contacts.

### What the assemblers do with an rDNA array

hifiasm builds a phased string graph from HiFi reads ([Cheng et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-020-01056-5)). It phases with either parental k-mers or Hi-C ([Cheng et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41587-022-01261-x)), and in its UL mode it adds a second, ultra-long-read graph to resolve repeats ([Cheng et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-024-02269-8)). Verkko builds a multiplex de Bruijn graph from accurate reads and simplifies it with ultra-long read paths and haplotype markers ([Rautiainen et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41587-023-01662-6)). Verkko2 adds Hi-C phasing and a scaffolder designed for the acrocentric tangle ([Antipov et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1101/gr.280383.124)).

Graph assemblers cannot linearise an array of near-identical units longer than any read. In the CHM13 string graph the five arrays formed five dense tangles ([Nurk et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.abj6987)). In HG002, Verkko resolved most chromosomes as separate components, but the acrocentrics were joined through their rDNA; chromosome-specific variants can partly separate tangles, as when Verkko isolated the chr15 and chr22 rDNA of CHM13 ([Rautiainen et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41587-023-01662-6)).

When a tangle is turned into contigs, rDNA can become a gap, a unit collapsed onto a contig end, a small unplaced contig or a scattered multi-copy representation. Rautiainen assembled the CHM13 reads with hifiasm and Verkko and searched the contigs for the known CHM13 morphs (distinct full-length unit sequences); neither assembler resolved the arrays. Verkko lacked any contig containing two of the morphs and placed the chr13 morph in seven locations. hifiasm placed the chr14 morph (12 true copies) in seven locations and the chr21a morph (15 true copies) in only one ([Rautiainen 2024](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btae124)). hifiasm (UL) behaves the same way outside humans: in *Arabidopsis* it assembled chromosomes 2 and 4 except for their rDNA-bearing short arms, and almost all its small contigs aligned to rDNA or chloroplast DNA ([Cheng et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-024-02269-8)). Verkko2 can also screen user-specified sequences out of the main assembly, and KY962518 is its default rDNA screen ([Antipov et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1101/gr.280383.124)). The rDNA content of a haplotype FASTA therefore also depends on post-processing choices.

## How complete genomes have handled rDNA

### T2T-CHM13: model arrays

Even in the effectively haploid CHM13, the rDNA was not assembled by the general method. The consortium built a sparse de Bruijn graph per array, split aligned nanopore reads into units, clustered them into morphs, estimated morph copy number from read support and polished with HiFi. The chr14 and chr22 arrays each consisted of one primary morph. The chr13, chr15 and chr21 arrays were mosaics the reads could not order, so their primary morphs were placed in consecutive blocks sized by estimated copy number and are model sequence. The assembly contains 219 complete rDNA copies over 9.9 Mb ([Nurk et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.abj6987)), close to half the ddPCR diploid estimate of 409, as expected for a homozygous genome; FISH-based per-array quantification helped set these sizes ([Potapova et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xgen.2025.101031)). CHM13's arrays are thus calibrated in composition and length but not in unit order.

### T2T-HG002 (Q100): sized gaps

The diploid HG002 benchmark is telomere-to-telomere for all 46 chromosomes except the interior of the rDNA arrays ([Hansen et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2026.06.016)); v1.1 keeps the centres of nine of ten arrays as N-gaps ([marbl/HG002](https://github.com/marbl/HG002)). In the preprint text, these FISH-sized gaps total 31.1 Mb, the one resolved array (paternal chr13) spans 275.9 kb, the scaffolds hold 49 rDNA copies, 100 ribotin morphs are supplied as unplaced contigs, and all rDNA is flagged low-confidence, making up 86.3% of low-confidence bases ([Hansen et al. 2025 preprint](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.09.21.677443)). The resolved chr13 array has six full units plus a partial one ([Potapova et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xgen.2025.101031)).

### Automated acrocentric assembly and targeted arrays

Verkko2 moved the acrocentrics from unassembled to scaffolded, not to complete. It resolved 55% of the acrocentric chromosomes tested as T2T scaffolds, typically leaving only the rDNA array as a gap. Across 17 HPRC trio samples it scaffolded 93 acrocentric chromosomes across the rDNA gap without introducing haplotype switches ([Antipov et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1101/gr.280383.124)). The largest short-arm study to date assembled 156 phased short arms from 16 pedigree members with Verkko2. Its authors describe the rDNA as "a predictable collapse region in each acrocentric assembly". Hi-C joined distal and proximal sequence in only 41% (64/156) of these short arms ([Lin et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2026.05.035)).

Complete arrays have been assembled only for small arrays or with targeted effort. Two very short arrays in HPRC assemblies, each with two full units, agreed with FISH estimates ([Potapova et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xgen.2025.101031)). In the T2T pedigree, a targeted assembly used nanopore ultra-long reads enriched by adaptive sampling and restricted to reads of at least 52 kb. It reconstructed one complete array, the paternal chr14, with 33 full units over 1.4 Mb, supported by spanning reads in every 100-kb window. The other arrays in that pedigree stayed incomplete ([Cechova et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.64898/2025.12.14.693655)). This is a preprint.

### Morph-level reconstruction with ribotin

Ribotin reconstructs unit variants instead of arrays. It recruits HiFi reads (via a reference unit or Verkko's rDNA tangles), builds an allele graph, threads ultra-long reads through it, extracts one loop per unit and clusters loops into morphs, reporting a consensus and an ultra-long read coverage per morph. Ribotin recovered every CHM13 major morph, with residual error rates of 0.2–0.4%. In simulation, morph coverage correlated with true copy count at 0.94, with 87% sensitivity and specificity. It also showed that a genome-wide consensus of all units is a mosaic that matches no unit actually present in the genome ([Rautiainen 2024](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btae124)). Ribotin reports unit diversity and relative abundance, not array order or array length.

## HPRC releases 1 and 2

Release 1 contained 94 trio-phased hifiasm haplotypes from 47 individuals ([Liao et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-05896-x)), built without ultra-long data ([Antipov et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1101/gr.280383.124)). The consortium described them as "almost free of rDNA arrays", and its HiFi-coverage error caller, Flagger, flagged CHM13's modelled rDNA as falsely duplicated in its entirety, so coverage-based evaluation is unreliable there. Of 217 putative inter-chromosomal joins, all but one involved acrocentric short arms. rDNA arrays, together with other satellites and transition regions, made up about 40% of the reference bases that the assemblies did not cover ([Liao et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-05896-x)). Only nine q-arm-anchored release 1 contigs appeared to cross an rDNA array, and Guarracino et al. did not expect those crossings to be correct ([Guarracino et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-05976-y)).

Release 2 was announced in May 2025 and covers 232 individuals ([HPRC r2 announcement](https://humanpangenome.org/hprc-data-release-2/)). Its assembly index has 466 rows. Of these, 452 haplotypes were assembled with hifiasm (versions 0.19.7–0.19.9) and 12 with Verkko; 256 rows are trio-phased and 208 Hi-C-phased ([HPRC r2 assembly index](https://github.com/human-pangenomics/hprc_intermediate_assembly/blob/main/data_tables/assemblies_release2_v1.0.index.csv)). The companion preprint describes hifiasm with HiFi plus ultra-long input, trio phasing for 126 of 230 samples and Hi-C for 104, plus an experimental Verkko set for all samples aimed at chrY and the acrocentric short arms. Only 157 acrocentric short arms (6.8%) were assembled contiguously without structural QC flags, the p-arms are "still typically not assembled accurately", and the T2T classification discards alignments lying entirely in the p-arm up to and including the rDNA ([Lucas et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.21.739710)). An acrocentric labelled T2T in r2 therefore says nothing about the completeness of its rDNA.

HPRC CenSat annotations come from the alphaAnnotation workflow, which r2's HMM-Flagger also uses ([Lucas et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.21.739710)). Its rDNA step scans each assembly with an rDNA profile HMM (nhmmer), merges hits within 50 kb and labels each merged interval "rDNA" ([alphaAnnotation cenSat workflow](https://github.com/kmiga/alphaAnnotation/tree/main/cenSatAnnotation)). A CenSat "rDNA" interval therefore marks rDNA-like sequence, not a whole array as opposed to a collapsed unit or fragments, and N-gaps are not labelled.

## Synthesis: expectations for HPRC r2

The literature supports four expectations for HPRC r2 haplotypes. Each is a prior to test, not a finding.

1. *Present, but not as arrays.* Most haplotypes should contain some 45S sequence at p-arm contig ends, in small unplaced contigs and in short resolved arrays, since hifiasm spreads morphs across contigs without regard to copy count.
2. *Rarely complete.* Gap-free arrays should be confined to arrays of a few units, which are real (about 4% of arrays carry 0–2 copies); curated diploid references still leave nine of ten arrays as gaps.
3. *Often unplaced or ambiguously placed.* Scaffolding across the rDNA is the hardest phasing step, the r2 T2T criterion ignores the p-arm through the rDNA, and heterologous exchange in the flanking PHRs complicates chromosome assignment.
4. *Not proportional to true copy number.* About 315 diploid units is roughly 7 Mb of 45S per haplotype (157 units × 45 kb), yet curated HG002 scaffolds hold 49 units in total. Assembled counts should sit far below true copy number, and any correlation is more likely to reflect which short arrays happen to resolve than proportional sampling.

Several observable features separate a faithful array from a collapsed or templated one:

- **Flank continuity.** A faithful array is bounded on the same contig, without Ns, by DJ sequence on one side and PJ sequence on the other, with the chromosome's own q-arm reachable through the proximal side.
- **Depth.** Depth across the array should match haplotype depth; a collapse shows an excess proportional to the missing copies. Read depth directly, since coverage-based flaggers misbehave here.
- **Spanning-read tiling.** Every window of about 100 kb should be spanned by ultra-long reads that carry rare, unit-distinguishing k-mers, as in the pedigree chr14 validation.
- **Unit heterogeneity.** Real arrays show chromosome-specific variants and structured morph diversity, including IGS repeat-length variants; long runs of byte-identical units, units identical to KY962518 or unordered morph blocks suggest modelling or templating.
- **Independent size agreement.** Assembled unit counts should agree with an orthogonal per-array estimate (FISH, or a share of the ddPCR or k-mer total) and, summed over ten arrays, approach the short-read genome-wide copy number.

## Claims not verified or modified

- *Parks et al. 2018 (315 ± 104 copies).* The DOI resolves, but the number was taken from its citation in [Nurk et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.abj6987). The Parks paper itself was not read.
- *Floutsakou et al. 2013 and van Sluis et al. 2019 on DJ/PJ architecture.* Both DOIs resolve (10.1101/gr.157941.113; 10.1101/gad.331892.119), but their contents were not read, so neither is cited. DJ and PJ statements rest on Nurk et al. 2022, Kim et al. 2021 and Lin et al. 2026.
- *Stults et al.* The Genome Research paper is dated 2008 (vol. 18) but was published online in 2007. Its ">10% per meiosis" rate is in tension with the later FISH-based pedigree stability. That conflict is reported above, not resolved.
- *HG002 details.* The 31.1 Mb gap total, 275.9 kb chr13 array, 49 scaffolded copies and 100 morphs were read in the bioRxiv preprint text. The Cell version (2026) was confirmed only at abstract level.
- *CenSat.* The "rDNA" labelling rule was read from the current alphaAnnotation workflow code. It was not confirmed that the r2 CenSat BED files were produced with this exact version, HMM profile or 50-kb merge distance.
- *Assembler post-processing.* Verkko2's KY962518 screening is documented. Whether the 12 r2 Verkko haplotypes, or the experimental Verkko set, were screened this way was not verified. hifiasm's rDNA behaviour in human HPRC assemblies is inferred from the CHM13 test in the ribotin paper and from the release 1 description. No hifiasm paper documents it directly.
- *Counts.* The trio/Hi-C split differs slightly between the r2 index (256/208 haplotype rows, which would be 128/104 samples) and the preprint (126/104 of 230 samples). Two of the 466 index rows have no assembler annotation. Neither discrepancy was investigated.
