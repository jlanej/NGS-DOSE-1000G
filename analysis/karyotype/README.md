# The direct tests behind docs/KARYOTYPE.md

Run from the repository root with the `ngsdose` library installed (0.3.0 or later) and `docs/` regenerated by
`regenerate.sh`: the scripts read the cached estimates (`cache/scan`, `cache/karyotype`, `cache/fetch`) and read
the cohort as the page does, from a genome's counts with the karyotype windows where it has them and from its scan
where it has not, against one model learned on all of them (`common.py`, which keeps that reading in
`cache/karyotype.analysis.pkl`; the first script to run makes it, in about three minutes). The outputs quoted
in `docs/KARYOTYPE.md` are in `out/`.

| script | question | answer on this cohort |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `fixed_model.py` | is a genome's reading its own, or the cohort's? | a model learned on the 2,504 genomes of the first release, applied unchanged to the 698 added later, writes 691 of their 698 karyotypes as the whole cohort's model does; the other seven differ in a share's last digit or by a stretch at the threshold, and the levels by 0.0004 to 0.0021 copies (0.05 to 0.12 of their standard error) |
| `checks.py` | what does the chain call where nothing can be real; does lower depth make calls; does the fetch read as the scan? | with the places of every chromosome in random order, 1,500 readings with the windows and 1,500 without give no stretch and no chromosome; counting noise added as at 30x to 4x widens the errors (noise factor 1.04 to 2.74, z's robust SD 0.97 to 1.02) and calls one chromosome in 750 readings; the fetch-mode counts give 3,202 of 3,202 karyotypes as the scan's counts of the same regions do |
| `spike_in.py` | what do the calls make of a chromosome or an arm gained or lost in part of the cells? | put into 125 clean genomes with the windows: a whole chromosome found in 84 to 100% of them at 5% of the cells (chromosome 22: 66%) and in all at 8%, an arm of 41 to 84 regions from 8 to 12%, an X lost in a woman from 8%, an X gained or a Y lost or gained in a man from 5%, each at the share put in; with the `screen` set, whole autosomes from 8 to 12% and the sex chromosomes from 5 to 8% |
| `allele_balance.py` | do the alleles of the same reads say what depth says? | over 51 changes in 5% of the cells or more in 68 genomes the share of the cells by the alleles agrees with depth's at r = 0.983 (robust SD of the difference 0.036); losses to one copy hold no heterozygous sites or hold them at the minority's share; two partial gains, NA12341's 4q (0.19 by depth, 0.05 by the alleles) and HG00703's 9q (0.15, 0.01), are not borne out |

`allele_calls.sh` makes the variant calls `allele_balance.py` reads: bcftools on the regions of the bundle's
`controls.bed`, in the small CRAM of one genome's containers. `slice_fetch.py` makes that CRAM: it fetches exactly
the containers of a remote CRAM that hold a set of regions, by byte range, and writes them as a valid CRAM (htslib's
own reads of a remote file moved 3.7 times the bytes it decoded here). It is how the 186 genomes of
`counts_karyotype/` were counted, about 450 MB and a minute each:

```bash
python3 analysis/karyotype/slice_fetch.py "$URL" "$SAMPLE.crai" "$NGSDOSE/resources/GRCh38/controls.bed" "$SAMPLE.cram" \
    --fai GRCh38_full_analysis_set_plus_decoy_hla.fa.fai
samtools index "$SAMPLE.cram"
ngs-dose count -m fetch -i "$SAMPLE.cram" -T GRCh38_full_analysis_set_plus_decoy_hla.fa -p "$B/panel.k31.tsv.gz" --classes rDNA5S \
    -c "$B/controls.fa.gz" --sinks "$B/sinks.bed" -s "$SAMPLE" -o "counts_karyotype/$SAMPLE.json.gz"     # B: the bundle
```

The container fetch reads the 5S rDNA's sinks too when they are in the BED (`cat controls.bed` with the 5S rows
of `sinks.bed`); `--reuse` adds regions to a CRAM made earlier without fetching its containers again.
